Tigers

Tigers (panthera tigris) are the largest cats in the world, weighing as much as 300kg and measuring as long as 11 feet. Classified as a threatened species, their range is just 5% of its historical size.



Quick Facts

  • It is estimated that there are just 5600 tigers in the wild.
  • A century ago, there were more than 100,000 tigers in the wild.
  • Tigers are native to Asia, with the majority being found in (the bengal tiger is the national animal of India) and Southeast Asia or Siberia.
  • Conservation efforts have helped to increase the population of tiger from a historic low of 3,200 in 2010.
  • There are more tigers in captivity in the US than there are in the wild.
  • Tigers can reach speeds of up to 40mph.
  • Tigers are the largest species of cats in the world.
  • Tigers are carnivores, preying on animals such as deer, wild boar, and antelope.
  • Tigers have a lifespan of between 10-15 years on average, but can live up to 20 years in captivity.
