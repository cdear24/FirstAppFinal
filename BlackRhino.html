Black Rhino

The black rhinoceros (diceros bicornis) is a species of African rhino. Smaller than the white rhino, they were once found throughout most of sub-Saharan Africa but are now critically endangered due to poaching.



Quick Facts

  • Despite their name, black rhinos actually have grey skin.
  • A black rhino can have skin up to 5cm thick.
  • Black rhinos are native to Africa and can be found in countries such as Kenya, Namibia, South Africa, and Swaziland.
  • The average lifespan of a black rhino is between 40 and 50 years.
  • Although smaller than the white rhino, black rhinos can reach weight of up to 1300kg and a height of up to 1.6m.
  • A black rhino can run at speeds betwen 30 and 40mph.
  • Black rhinos are critically endangered today, having been victims of poaching.
  • Their estimated population in 1995 was less than 2500 but conservation efforts have increased the population to more than 6000 today.
  • Black rhinos are herbivores, feeding on leaves, twigs, branches and fruit.
