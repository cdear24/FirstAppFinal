Capybara

Native to South America, capybaras (Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris) are the world's largest rodents. They are known for being exceptional swimmers and inhabit areas near freshwater sources.



Quick Facts

  • Capybaras can grow to be up to 1.2m long and 0.6m tall.
  • Capbybaras have teeth which grow continuously.
  • Capybaras' toes are partially webbed, which helps them to swim.
  • The average lifespan of a capybara in the wild is 7 years.
  • An adult capybara can weigh up to 65kg (the same as a wolf).
  • Capybaras are herbivores, feeding on aquatic plants, grasses, and bark which also helps to wear down their teeth.
  • There are an estimated 3.7 million capybaras, with 1.2 million of those living in Brazil and are not currently endangered.
  • Capybaras are very social animals and typically live in groups of up to 40.
  • Capybaras are able to walk and swim within a few hours of birth.
